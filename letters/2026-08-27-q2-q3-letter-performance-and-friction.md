## I. The Sweet Spot: USDC Smart Market Capture

Ted Williams, the great baseball hitter, used to explain that the secret to a .400 batting average was waiting for a pitch in the sweet spot of his strike zone. In investing, unlike baseball, there are no called strikes. You can stand at the plate and watch dozens of pitches pass by until you see one right down the middle.

Our **USDC Market Capture (Smart)** strategy turned out to be one of those pitches.

Participating in automated market making is one of the genuine sources of cash-flow yield in digital assets—earning direct fees whenever market participants trade. However, conventional market-making approaches in crypto are often static and passive, leaving capital vulnerable to abrupt market shifts and eroding net returns during volatile periods.

We approached market making with proprietary models. By systematically analyzing volatility regimes and volume dynamics, our systems dynamically adapt our liquidity positioning to maintain optimal fee capture while managing downside exposure.

The result has exceeded our conservative internal forecasts. Even during periods of sharp intraday choppy trading, the strategy kept collecting fees and compounding its price-per-share yield steadily. We will not publish our exact parameters and execution thresholds—doing so would invite copycats to crowd the trade and dilute the returns for our partners. But we are delighted to report that the core thesis has proven sound in live market conditions.

## II. Battle-Testing Market Making: From Volatility Shocks to 24/7 Automation

Now for the other side of the ledger.

Our **USDC Market Making** strategy was initially designed around statistical optimization of liquidity spreads. In normal market conditions, the model operated cleanly, capturing an annualized gross yield in the neighborhood of 6% to 8% APY *(a point-in-time figure that varies dynamically based on trading volumes, liquidity depth, and market volatility)*. 

However, live markets are the ultimate crucible. During a recent sharp market dislocation—a sudden price swing that created a localized black-swan shock—the rapid market move pushed our position outside its optimal range. 

Fortunately, because of our conservative risk parameters, **we suffered only minimal profit loss, and partner principal remained completely secure.** 

Nevertheless, the event delivered an invaluable operational lesson: in digital asset markets that trade around the clock, relying on periodic statistical calibrations or manual interventions leaves a strategy vulnerable to sudden volatility spikes. When market dynamics pivot in seconds, human reaction time becomes a point of friction.

Did we abandon the strategy or paper over the numbers? Absolutely not. We used the live test to harden our architecture. We immediately re-engineered the process into a fully automated, 24/7 execution engine. The upgraded system continuously monitors volatility and depth in real time, adapting positions autonomously with zero lag and minimal personal intervention.

Facing market friction transparently and engineering permanent automated safeguards is how a serious fund protects capital.

## III. Evaluating Permissioned Yield: The Case of Steakhouse Turbo USDC

A foundational rule of our partnership is that we will not compromise on our ability to protect partner principal and exit positions cleanly without capital loss.

We have utilized Steakhouse as one of our baseline strategies for reliable lending yield across our portfolio. The newer **USDC Steakhouse Turbo** version was designed to amplify returns—achieving attractive headline yields in the 8% to 13% range by routing deposited capital into specialized, higher-utilization lending markets and leveraged collateral-looping pairs rather than standard broad-market pools. 

However, along with this aggressive yield engine, the underlying architecture introduced a **permissioned whitelist**.

Our approach to permissioned mechanisms is pragmatic rather than dogmatic: **we evaluate permissioning through a rigorous risk lens centered on capital preservation and exit safety.**
* **Entry-Gated Whitelists:** If a whitelist applies solely to initial entry or institutional compliance onboarding, while redemption and exit rights remain mathematically open, unencumbered, and protected against capital loss, we can still consider the strategy based on its specific terms and risk-reward profile.
* **Exit Risk & Discretionary Frictions:** If a permissioned structure introduces ambiguity around exit liquidity, rebalancing access, or third-party gatekeeping that could impair our ability to withdraw funds with zero capital loss, it crosses our risk boundary.

When we evaluated the terms and whitelist architecture of the Turbo version at the time, the potential operational and exit frictions did not meet our strict safety thresholds. Rather than accepting uncompensated risk on the exit door, we proactively rotated our capital out of the Turbo strategy with our principal completely intact, reallocating it to battle-tested blue-chip strategies.

We will continue to evaluate permissioned and semi-permissioned strategies as their terms mature, but our standard remains firm: we only participate when our fund's ability to exit cleanly and without capital loss is fully assured.

## IV. The Fortress: RLUSD and Morpho Yearn Allocations

At Berkshire Hathaway, Warren Buffett always insists on holding a massive fortress of ultra-safe Treasury bills—not because they provide thrilling returns, but because they ensure the enterprise can sleep soundly through any financial tempest.

We take the exact same approach with our **bedrock allocations**:

### 1. Curve RLUSD/USDC Strategy
This strategy pairs **RLUSD**—the enterprise-grade, regulated stablecoin issued by Ripple on the XRP Ledger ecosystem—with **USDC** inside Curve's liquidity pool. It captures transaction fees from institutional and retail dollar settlements. The peg risk is minimal, the counterparties are among the most solvent in digital assets, and the return is steady as a clock.

### 2. Morpho Yearn OG USDC Compounder
This strategy allocates capital through **Morpho**—a peer-to-peer lending protocol that optimizes loan efficiency—and uses Yearn's battle-tested infrastructure to compound the returns automatically. It is as close to an institutional cash deposit as exists in the decentralized world.

These two bedrock strategies will never produce headlines on social media, and they will never win a contest for the highest projected APY. Their job is to sit quietly in the vault, compound day in and day out, and provide a rock-solid foundation while we deploy more active strategies elsewhere.

They are doing their job with distinction.
